# Erasure-coding dress rehearsal — Lyve + Geyser + OneDrive fleet, 2026-09-24

Phase 11 (Reed-Solomon across backends) is not implemented. This is the
driver-level rehearsal: `cmd/erasure-bench` RS(10,6)-encodes a random payload,
PUTs all 16 shards in parallel through the real drivers, then reads it back
three ways and verifies the SHA-256 every time. Run from slc-vaultaire-01
(`~/vaultaire-bench/erasure-bench`, creds from `.env.bench`, Geyser LA bucket,
Lyve us-west-1 service key, 3 OneDrive tenants). Raw logs on the box:
`~/vaultaire-bench/ec-*.log`.

Read shapes:

- **first-10** — all 16 shards requested at once, first 10 to land win, rest cancelled (the real read path).
- **lose:X** — every shard on backend X treated as lost; rebuild from survivors (backend outage).

## Layout A — Lyve 6 data, Geyser 4 data, OneDrive 6 parity

| Payload | Encode | PUT wall (payload / wire MB/s) | first-10 | lose:geyser | lose:lyve | lose:onedrive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 MiB (×2) | 5–11 ms | 3.3–3.7 s (17–20 / 27–31) | 0.9 s, 67–71 MB/s | 1.3–1.6 s, 41–50 | 1.5 s, 41–42 | 2.3–2.4 s, 27–28 |
| 256 MiB (×2) | 20 ms | 4.7–5.8 s (44–55 / 70–88) | 1.2–1.7 s, 146–211 MB/s | 1.4–1.5 s, 172–185 | 6.0–6.7 s, 38–43 | 5.0–6.1 s, 42–51 |
| 1 GiB (×1) | 82 ms | 17.4 s (59 / 94) | 6.0 s, 170 MB/s | 9.2 s, 112 | 27.4 s, 37 | 23.6 s, 43 |

Every read: hash ok. Decode cost 30–700 ms (≈1.5 GB/s), encode ≈12 GB/s —
both invisible next to the network.

## What the numbers say

1. **Writes are gated by OneDrive.** In every run the slowest shard was a
   OneDrive shard (3.3 s at 6.4 MiB → 17.4 s at 102 MiB, ≈6 MB/s per shard,
   ≈35 MB/s across 6 parallel shards). Lyve finished its shards in ≤1.5 s and
   Geyser in ≤2 s. Client-visible PUT throughput of an erasure-coded object
   therefore tops out around **60 MB/s** at 1 GiB, i.e. the fleet is the
   write floor. A synchronous erasure PUT that includes OneDrive is a
   3–5× slowdown versus today's single-backend PUT (Lyve alone did 161 MB/s
   in the #400 bench). Parity to OneDrive has to be **asynchronous** — write
   data shards synchronously, parity in the background — exactly the
   "async second-copy" role the permafrost memory already assigns it.
2. **Reads are fast and the race picks the right shards.** first-10 never
   waited on Geyser at 256 MiB+ (winners = Lyve 6 + OneDrive 4). 170–211 MB/s
   client-visible at 1 GiB, better than any single backend's GET from SLC
   except Lyve paired runs. OneDrive *reads* are respectable (4 shards of
   25.6 MiB in ≈1.2 s ≈ 85 MB/s aggregate) even though its writes crawl.
3. **Geyser is the read bottleneck when it is needed.** lose:lyve forces 4
   (A) or 6 (B) Geyser shards into the read: 6–8 s at 256 MiB, 27 s at 1 GiB
   (≈15 MB/s per shard). And this is Geyser's **disk cache** — the shards
   were written seconds earlier. Once they age to tape, a Geyser-dependent
   read means a RestoreObject and minutes (LA1 ≤3 min), not seconds.
   Consequence: Geyser shards are parity-only and count as "durable but
   slow" — never in the happy path, only in the outage path, and the
   outage path is a restore, not a GET.
4. **Any single backend can die.** All three lose:X reads reconstruct
   correctly in both layouts because no backend holds more than 6 shards.
   Lose Lyve and you are reading from Geyser+OneDrive at ~40 MB/s: degraded
   but serving.
5. **CPU is free.** RS(10,6) encodes at 12 GB/s and reconstructs at ~1.5 GB/s
   on the SLC box; a 1 GiB object costs 82 ms to encode. Memory: the bench
   holds the whole payload + 1.6× shards in RAM; the engine version must
   stream per chunk (Phase 11.3 puts erasure after chunking, so shards are
   ≤64 MiB / 10 anyway).

## What this changes in the Phase 11 plan

- The plan's launch placement (6 data own-fleet, 4 data iDrive, 2 parity
  Geyser, 4 parity OneDrive) is consistent with these numbers: data shards on
  fast backends, parity on Geyser + OneDrive. Until the own fleet exists,
  **iDrive/Lyve carry all 10 data shards**; Geyser and OneDrive share the 6
  parity shards.
- Add to 11.3: **parity shards are written asynchronously** (queue + retry),
  synchronous PUT commits when the 10 data shards land. Otherwise every
  customer PUT inherits OneDrive's ~6 MB/s per-shard ceiling.
- Add to 11.6 (self-healing): a Geyser-resident shard is only "available"
  after RestoreObject; the repair job must issue restores, not GETs.
- first-k with cancellation is the right read primitive; the bench shows it
  costs nothing to over-request (cancelled fetches are cheap) and it
  automatically routes around a slow backend.

## Caveats

- Geyser reads = disk cache (fresh writes). Not representative of tape-cold
  reads. Lyve single runs are noisy (paired runs shown). OneDrive fleet = 3
  tenants here (prod has more), FNV placement — no guarantee shards land on
  distinct tenants, so the "OneDrive never holds enough to reconstruct"
  privacy property was not exercised.
- Bench allocates the full payload in memory; sized for ≤1 GiB.
- Nothing touches the engine; all shards deleted after each run (Lyve prefix
  `ecbench/`, Geyser `ecbench/ec-*`, OneDrive `ec-*` — verified empty).

Repro: `set -a; . ~/vaultaire-bench/.env.bench; set +a; GEYSER_BUCKET=$GEYSER_LA_BUCKET ./erasure-bench -mb 256 -runs 2 [-layout "lyve:6,onedrive:4,geyser:6"]`.
