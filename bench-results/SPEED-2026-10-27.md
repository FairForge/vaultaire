## The answer

1. **The edge has no upload ceiling worth worrying about. The ~150 MB/s of 2026-10-26 was a client running 8 streams, not a Worker limit.**
   - Through the pass-through Worker from SLC: 375 MB/s at 8 parts in flight, 636 at 16 and 838 at 32 to Lyve; 978 MB/s at 32 × 16 MiB.
   - iDrive flattens at ~550 from 16 parts.
   - One stream holds 35–50 MB/s wherever the backend is, Tokyo included. Placement by colo buys nothing.
2. **Reads through the Worker should fan in.**
   - One backend stream through the Worker gave 80 MB/s (Lyve) and 50 (iDrive).
   - Lyve: 8 presigned ranges piped in order into one `IdentityTransformStream` doubled that at 2 ms of CPU per 2 GiB.
   - iDrive: the same shape fails the rule. The window of 16 MiB ranges × 6 in flight doubles both backends, at ~1 s of CPU per GiB.
3. **A second copy at the edge is a tee, never Reed-Solomon.**
   - The tee runs at pass-through speed: 44 vs 44 MB/s per stream, 1 ms of CPU per 64 MiB.
   - RS at the edge runs at 26–29 MB/s per stream with either codec, Rust-SIMD or Go, at 4+2 or 8+4, with 0.74–0.92 s of CPU per 64 MiB. That is under the 100 MB/s stop rule: RS goes to the node or the async mover.
   - RaptorQ encodes at 39–49 MB/s per CPU-second in workerd, so F-11 stands.
4. **Geyser can be an EdgeWriter.** Vail enforces a presigned PUT's signed length and Content-MD5 or SHA-256 exactly as Lyve and iDrive do, and its multipart Complete takes 0.2–2 s. On 2026-10-27 a Worker moved bytes to Vail at only 12–16 MB/s per stream (direct from SLC: 25–33). **A rerun on 2026-10-30 (§ 12) did not reproduce that: Worker → Vail uploads ran level with direct from SLC**, so the network is no reason against an edge write. A GLACIER write must still be sealed, and sealing touches every byte. So Vault writes stay at the origin, later the node, and Vault reads stay at the origin.
5. **An edge-admitted aws-cli upload works at 245 MB/s from one client (64 MB × 16) with no part on SLC.** Admit ~3 ms and commit ~2 ms on the origin, the bytes MD5-exact (section 9, with `edge_ingest` on for the bench tenant).
6. **aws-cli's default upload can move at the edge without decoding** if the control plane signs the backend request as a header-signed `STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER` PUT. Both backends then store the decoded bytes. But Lyve does not check the trailer, and iDrive refuses aws-chunked on any presigned (query) link.

## 1. aws-chunked pass-through

**Captured shape.** Each aws-cli version PUT a body to a local TLS listener:
- aws-cli 2.35.21 on SLC, a 256 MiB `s3api put-object`;
- aws-cli 2.28.19 on the Mac, 16 MiB.

Both sent:

```
Transfer-Encoding: chunked            (HTTP chunked; NO Content-Length)
Content-Encoding: aws-chunked
X-Amz-Content-SHA256: STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER
X-Amz-Decoded-Content-Length: 268435456
X-Amz-Trailer: x-amz-checksum-crc64nvme     (CRC64NVME, not CRC32)
x-amz-sdk-checksum-algorithm: CRC64NVME
Expect: 100-continue
```

The body is 268,438,060 bytes:
- 256 chunks of 1 MiB, each `100000\r\n<1 MiB>\r\n`;
- then `0\r\n`, the trailer `x-amz-checksum-crc64nvme:0uUCWtO6mX4=\r\n` and `\r\n`.

The first bytes are `100000\r\n` followed by data; the last 200 bytes are data, then `\r\n0\r\nx-amz-checksum-crc64nvme:…\r\n\r\n`.

**Replays.** The same encoded body was sent unchanged, by curl, with `Content-Encoding: aws-chunked`, `x-amz-decoded-content-length`, `x-amz-trailer` and `x-amz-content-sha256: STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER`. The CRC32 rows re-frame the same chunks with a CRC32 trailer; the "bad" rows carry a wrong checksum.

| Link | Trailer | iDrive us-central-1 | Lyve us-west-1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| presigned, `host` only (UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD) | crc64nvme / crc32 | **400** AccessDenied "requires a valid Date or x-amz-date header" | **403** SignatureDoesNotMatch |
| presigned, the four aws-chunked headers signed, payload hash `UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD` | both | 400 (same) | 403 |
| presigned, the four headers signed, payload hash `STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER` | crc32 | 400 (same) | **200**, stored = decoded body (MD5 `a49152db…` = the file), ETag = that MD5 |
| **header-signed** (`Authorization` with `x-amz-date`, the four headers signed, `STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER`) | crc64nvme | **200**, stored 268,435,456 B = decoded, ETag = MD5 | **200**, the same |
| header-signed | crc32 | 200, decoded | 200, decoded |
| header-signed, **wrong** CRC32 / CRC64NVME | — | **400 XAmzContentChecksumMismatch** | **200, not checked**: the stored bytes are right because the body was |
| header-signed, body sent `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` (no Content-Length, as aws-cli sends it) | crc64nvme | 200, decoded | 200, decoded |

Control: a plain presigned PUT with `Content-Encoding: aws-chunked` and an ordinary body gets 200 on iDrive and **500 InternalError** on Lyve.

**Verdict.**
- **iDrive:** refused on any presigned (query) link. Accepted as-is with a header-signed request, which the control plane can mint, since it is a precomputed signature like a presign. The trailer is verified.
- **Lyve:** accepted as-is on a presign that signs the streaming payload hash and the aws-chunked headers, and with a header-signed request. The trailer is **not** verified.

Neither backend needs the Worker to decode. aws-cli also sends `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` with no Content-Length, and the Worker forwards a streamed body the same way: both backends take it.

## 4. Read fan-in (SLC ← Worker ← presigned GET, a 2 GiB object)

Every output was MD5-checked against the source (all equal).

Shapes:
- `fan-in N`: N concurrent ranged GETs of one presigned URL, each `await r.body.pipeTo(writable, {preventClose: true})` in order into one `IdentityTransformStream`, closed after the last.
- `window 16×6`: the 2026-10-04 § 16.2 shape. 16 MiB ranges, 6 in flight, each `arrayBuffer()` written in order to a `FixedLengthStream`.

| Shape | Lyve MB/s | iDrive MB/s | CPU ms per 2 GiB (tail) | TTFB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| direct presigned GET from SLC | 251 | 122 | — | 0.10–0.15 s |
| control: one presigned GET streamed by the Worker | **80** (77 / 72 / 80) | **50** | 0–1 | 0.10–0.43 s |
| fan-in 4 | 152 (135 / 137 / 152) | 71 | 2–3 | 0.02–0.04 s |
| fan-in 8 | **159** (143 / 159 / 130) = **2.0×** | 44 = **0.9×** | 2–3 | 0.02–0.04 s |
| window 16 MiB × 6 | **162** = 2.0× | **99** = **2.0×** | ~2,000 | 0.02–0.03 s |

**Verdict:** `pipeTo` is native: 2–3 ms of CPU for 2 GiB.
- Lyve: the fan-in passes the 1.5× rule at 4 and at 8 ranges.
- iDrive: it does not (1.4× at 4, 0.9× at 8). A Worker has six open connections, and ranges that wait their turn stall behind TCP back-pressure on a backend that is slow per connection.
- The window shape doubles both backends at ~1 s of CPU per GiB, so a 30 GiB read needs `limits.cpu_ms` above the 30 s default.

RB-1 is answered: build the windowed reader, 16 MiB ranges with ≤ 6 in flight. Use plain in-order `pipeTo` with 4 ranges only where the backend is Lyve.

## 10. Erasure coding at the edge, the WRITE side

**Codecs.**
- Rust: `tools/edge-ec-worker/codec` with `rs_encode` (in place: data slots → parity slots, `reed-solomon-erasure` 6 GF(2^8)) and `rq_encode` (`raptorq` 2) added. Built for `wasm32-unknown-unknown` with `RUSTFLAGS=-C target-feature=+simd128` (313 KB) and without it (308 KB).
- Go: klauspost/reedsolomon v1.14.0 built `GOOS=js GOARCH=wasm` (2.9 MB, `wasm_exec.js` glue, `js.CopyBytesToGo/JS`).
- The codec change stayed in the scratchpad; this PR is docs-only.

**(a)(b) The pipeline.**
- Input: a 1 GiB body as 16 × 64 MiB requests, one stream, sequential. One request cannot carry it: the 100 MB body limit.
- Per request: the Worker reads the body, cuts 8 MiB stripes, encodes k+m shards per stripe in WASM, and PUTs each shard to its own presigned Lyve URL (k+m links per stripe in an `x-links` JSON header: 48 links for 4+2, 96 for 8+4, ≈ 46 KB of header, accepted), with ≤ 4 stripes in flight.
- All 16 × 3 runs answered 200 with every shard 200.

| Shape | Per-stream MB/s (3 runs) | CPU ms per 64 MiB, p50 (tail) | Worker wall ms per 64 MiB |
|---|---|---|---|
| RS 4+2, Rust-SIMD | 26.1 / 28.5 / **28.8** | 761 | 1,967 |
| RS 8+4, Rust-SIMD | 25.9 / 25.6 / 25.5 | 919 | 2,180 |
| RS 4+2, Go | 27.6 / **28.3** / 27.6 | 744 | 1,954 |
| RS 8+4, Go | 26.2 / 26.4 / 26.4 | 848 | 2,107 |
| **mirror tee** (`req.body.tee()`, each branch `pipeThrough(FixedLengthStream)`, two PUTs) | **44.2** / 42.3 / 41.8 | **1** | 1,110 |
| control: pass-through, one PUT | 43.1 / 44.0 | 0 | — |

**(c) Isolate memory in `wrangler dev`** (port 8797, the Mac, one 64 MiB `/ec` request):

| | WASM linear memory | workerd process RSS before → peak |
|---|---|---|
| Rust 4+2 | 13.8 MB (one 12 MiB stripe slot) | 36 → 239 MB |
| Rust 8+4 | 26.5 MB (both slots) | 240 → 271 MB |
| Go 4+2 / 8+4 | Go's own heap (not exposed) | 271 → 408 / 401 MB |

The process RSS is all of workerd, not the metered isolate. On the edge none of the 112 `/ec` requests hit the 128 MB isolate limit (outcome `ok` on every one). The only `exceededMemory`-class failure seen (a 500 / 1104) was the first build allocating a stripe slot per request without freeing it, fixed by one slot per (k, m).

**(d) The read, one data shard withheld.** The Worker fetches k shards per stripe (k−1 data + parity 0) with 2 stripes prefetched, `rs_reconstruct` in WASM (Rust-SIMD), and streams the data out through a `FixedLengthStream`. 1 GiB = 16 × 64 MiB, MD5 of every 64 MiB equal to the source (16/16 on every run).

| | MB/s (3 runs) | CPU ms per 64 MiB, p50 |
|---|---|---|
| RS 4+2, data shard 1 withheld | 71 / **90** / 87 | 219 |
| RS 8+4, data shard 3 withheld | 81 / 78 / **83** | 230 |

**The stop rule.** Every encoder shape runs under 100 MB/s per stream in workerd: 26–29 end to end, 120–324 MB/s per CPU-second for the encode alone. The pipeline is bound by JS touching every byte (≈ 0.75 s of CPU per 64 MiB, the 2026-10-26 lesson) and by the six open connections a Worker gets: 48–96 shard PUTs per 64 MiB queue six at a time. **So RS 4+2 and 8+4 are "node or async".** The Go and Rust codecs are equal at the edge, so the codec is not the problem.

**Verdicts:**
- **Second copy at the edge: tee.**
- **8+4: async.** It belongs to the node (Prompt 47) when one exists, else the async mover from the mirror.
- **RaptorQ at the edge: no, F-11 stands** (39–49 MB/s per CPU-second for the encode alone).

## 12. Rerun 2026-10-30: Worker → Geyser (correction to § 11)

§ 11(c)'s Worker → Vail cap (~25 MB/s in total, not growing with streams) was rerun to find whether the path or our Worker caused it. It did not reproduce.

Setup:
- A throwaway Worker `sb-geyser-1030` on the bench account (deleted after). Three routes: `/gen` makes the body in the Worker (no client upload leg), `/relay` forwards the client's body exactly as the production Worker does (`edge/s3-ingest/src/index.js`: `fetch(url, { body: request.body })`), `/down` reads and discards.
- Short-lived host-only presigned links minted on SLC; the Worker never held a key.
- Targets in the same minutes: Geyser LA2 (a `t-_lab/cf1030/` prefix in the prod Vault bucket), iDrive us-central-1 and Lyve us-east-1 as controls. 64 MiB bodies, 1 and 8 at a time, two runs each, plus the § 11 shape (one upload of 16 × 64 MiB parts, 8 in flight, then Complete).
- Triggered from SLC (colo SLC) and from the owner's Mac (colo DFW, the Worker generating the bytes).
- 269 objects written and deleted the same evening, long before the ~11:00 UTC flush; the prefix listed 0 keys and 0 uploads on all three backends after. Raw JSONL on SLC under `~/vaultaire-bench/cfgey-1030/`.

Results (Geyser figures given relative to direct):
- **Uploads, colo SLC:** through the Worker, generated or relayed, single PUTs and the 16-part multipart all ran **level with direct from SLC** (within run-to-run noise in both directions). Every part answered 200; every Complete answered 200 in seconds.
- **Uploads, colo DFW:** the same order as from SLC; the multipart completed clean.
- **Per stream** every path to Vail runs at the same modest rate. The limit is Vail's per-connection ingest, and parallel parts are the lever, as the origin driver already does.
- **Controls:** Lyve 434–499 MB/s generated at 8 (relay 159–178, the client leg), iDrive 220–597. Normal.
- **Reads through the Worker are still 2.5–4× slower than direct** (one plain `fetch` stream, 3.5–6 s to first byte). § 11(d)'s windowed reader stands.
- **Geyser's direct rate itself varies by day:** today's direct runs were below 2026-10-27's. One session is not a verdict on a backend.

Network facts:
- All three Geyser sites (LA1, LA2, LON1) are single-homed on Cogent (AS174), IPv4 only.
- Cloudflare (AS13335) and Cogent are direct neighbours (RIPEstat).
- SLC reaches Cogent inside Salt Lake City and stays on-net to LA (~31 ms).

**Verdict:**
- **The ~25 MB/s Worker → Vail cap of 2026-10-27 was a window, not a property** — of that afternoon's path or of Vail, never isolated.
- **Our Worker is not at fault:** the relayed shape is the production Worker's.
- **An edge write to Geyser is viable on the network.** What keeps GLACIER at the origin is the sealing (JS-touched bytes, § 10) and the master key (F-14), not the path.

## What changes

- **Admit rule:**
  - **No region-by-colo rule.** Per stream the Worker leg sets the speed, not the backend's distance (section 3: 0–7 %, noise).
  - The one region fact worth keeping: iDrive us-midwest-1 straggled on every run from SLC. Watch it before placing buckets there.
- **Worker changes:**
  1. **Fan-in reads (RB-1):** the windowed reader, 16 MiB ranges, ≤ 6 in flight (the Worker's connection limit), each `arrayBuffer()` written in order. It doubles both backends (Lyve 80 → 162, iDrive 50 → 99) at ~1 s of CPU per GiB, so `limits.cpu_ms` goes up with it. Plain in-order `pipeTo` of 4 ranges costs 2 ms but only helps Lyve.
  2. **aws-chunked pass-through (RE-2):**
     - Admit aws-cli's default shape with no decoder. For iDrive the control plane mints a **header-signed** `STREAMING-UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD-TRAILER` request (`Authorization` + `x-amz-date` + the four aws-chunked headers, signed); for Lyve that, or a presign signing the same.
     - The Worker forwards body and headers untouched, `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` included.
     - Lyve does **not** verify the trailer, so a Lyve-bound aws-chunked write has no checksum the backend enforces unless the client also sends `Content-MD5`. The admit should then either send it to iDrive or keep it at the origin until the control plane can name a verified digest.
     - aws-cli now sends **CRC64NVME**, not CRC32.
- **Second copy:** **Prompt 48 builds the mirror as a tee at the edge** (44 MB/s per stream, 1 ms of CPU per 64 MiB, the pass-through's speed). RS 4+2 and 8+4 go to the **node** (Prompt 47) or the **async mover** reading the mirror. At the edge they cost 26–29 MB/s per stream and 0.75–0.92 s of CPU per 64 MiB with either codec. RaptorQ is out (F-11).
- **GLACIER-class writes are not sealed whole at the edge.**
  - Bench 11 says Vail could take an edge write (it enforces the presign), but the shape it would need fails at the edge: one 0x06 stream sealed as it streams, with RS 4+4 parity computed alongside.
  - The reason that stands: bytes touched by JS run under 30 MB/s per stream (section 10). The second reason given here, a Worker → Vail stream at 12–16 MB/s, did not reproduce on 2026-10-30 (§ 12).
  - **GLACIER stays at the origin, the node after Prompt 47, sealed and with its parity computed there.**
- **Prod env and constants:**
  - The S3-class uploader goes to **32 MiB × 8**: +29 % Lyve, +42 % iDrive. It is a constant, so it changes in a code PR, and `S3SinglePutMaxBytes` moves with it.
  - `GEYSER_GET_CONCURRENCY` 32 → **64** (+81 % on a 4 GiB landing-zone read): an owner `.env` change through `vaultaire-switch restart`.
  - No `.env` was changed by this bench.
- **Client settings:** `docs/API.md` § "Fast uploads and downloads" (this PR).
- **R2 landing zone:** alive but not faster. It stays the shape for single PUTs over 100 MB and for a slow backend; it is not the default.
