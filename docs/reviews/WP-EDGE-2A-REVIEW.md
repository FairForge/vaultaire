## Verdict

**HOLDS at P2.** The cryptography is sound and pinned (format 0x06, the kind
cannot be presented as another format's, the AAD binds the wrap and the
version, the keyed hash binds the plaintext after the open; every tamper
class has a test; the WASM chunker is byte-identical to Go on the golden
body); the control plane produces `storeChunkedObject`'s rows and the harness
proves it row by row; nothing of the hierarchy travels but a public key; the
index stores keyed hashes only and the test sweeps rows AND logs. The flag is
OFF everywhere and prod has no key version, so nothing is exposed. The P2 is a
coverage gap, not a defect in what was built: the codec takes only CHUNKED
placements, which is not what the edge mostly carries (RP68-1). RP68-1, 2, 3,
5 and 6 are Prompt 46b's Part 0 — before the Worker 0.3.0 is built on this
admit shape, because 46b's pipeline and its oracle must be written to the
corrected eligibility rule, not re-written after.

## Findings

| # | Sev | Finding | Where | Fix (Prompt 46b Part 0 unless said) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RP68-1** | **P2** | **The codec covers only what the ORIGIN would chunk, which is not what the edge carries.** `edgeAdmitWrite` enters the codec only when `placement.Kind == placeChunked` — a whole PUT over the 64 MiB `chunkThreshold` — and a FIRST multipart part only when ITS OWN size places chunked. So with `edge_codec` ON: every 4–64 MiB PutObject, and every part of every aws-cli (8 MiB parts), rclone (5 MiB), s5cmd and SDK multipart upload under 64 MiB per part — the bulk of real uploads — takes Part A's whole path: plaintext at the backend, no dedup, no seal. Prompt 48's `standard_sealed_default` then has two bad outcomes: those writes pass through to the origin (every aws-cli multipart byte back through SLC — 40.10's point lost), or the sealed-default sentence is false at the edge. The prompt said "chunked-eligible" and the build read that as the origin's threshold; the edge needs its own rule. | `edge_ingest.go:612–634`; `edge_codec_multipart.go:66–71`; `placement.go:184` | **At the edge, a codec tenant's write is codec by the EDGE rule:** a whole PUT ≥ `EDGE_MIN_BYTES` and EVERY part of a multipart upload (S3's 5 MiB minimum is above the 4 MiB floor) when the bucket allows chunking (`!versioned`, `!byTier`, `!byRegion`, not SSE-C, the `chunking` flag) — the size term alone is dropped: add `placementRequest.EdgeCodec bool` that `decidePlacement` treats as "size over the threshold", so every other rule stays the one decision; the grant's `chunk_threshold` becomes `EDGE_MIN_BYTES`. The harness fixture already lowers the threshold; add the 4–64 MiB whole PUT and the 8 MiB-part upload to it and to the oracle (such an object is a CHUNKED row at the edge where the origin would store it whole or SSE-S3 — say so in the note and `ENCRYPTION.md`; reads do not care). |
| **RP68-2** | P3 | **Parallel parts of ONE upload that share a chunk fail each other's commit.** aws-cli uploads 10 parts at once. Part 5's index call finds part 1's YOUNG claim → answered HIT (D-EDGE-20); part 5 finishes first, its commit's `referenceEdgeChunk` needs `state = 'ready'` → `errEdgeChunkVanished` → 503 Retry-After 5 → aws-cli re-sends the whole part (its retry budget is 5 attempts with short backoff; a part that takes longer than that to commit on the other side fails the upload). Bodies with repeated regions — VM images, database dumps, zero runs — hit this on every upload. The harness proves the cross-admit race only. | `edge_codec_control.go:429–432` (the hit), `:616–637` (the reference) | The commit WAITS on a young claim before it fails: in `referenceEdgeChunk`, a `pending` row younger than the window is polled with `chunk_claim.go`'s backoff (25 ms → 1 s) for up to 60 s on the commit's own clock, then `errEdgeChunkVanished`; a dropped claim (the other admit failed) is a miss the retry stores. Test: two part admits of bodies sharing a chunk, the hit-holder commits first and succeeds once the claimer flips ready. |
| **RP68-3** | P3 | **A stored chunk's integrity rests on the host's word.** The commit records `backend_etag` as the host reports it and never asks the backend; the link signs only the length, so ANY bytes of that length land. A host that reports a wrong ETag, uploads a body the backend accepted but that is not the sealed chunk, or hashed the wrong plaintext, gets a PUT 200 whose object is unreadable for ever (the GET fails closed — correct, but a silent total loss), and a dedup hit on such a chunk breaks every later object of the tenant holding those bytes. The origin's chunked path never had this exposure (it wrote the bytes). Part B puts a new, young codebase (the Worker 0.3.0) in that seat. | `edge_codec_control.go:538–560`; `s3_engine_adapter.go:1711–1752` (where it surfaces) | (a) The commit HEADs a SAMPLE of the stored chunks on the backend — the first stored chunk of every commit and 1 in 32 after it — and refuses the commit (everything given back) on an ETag or length mismatch (`vaultaire_edge_codec_chunks_total{outcome="head_mismatch"}`); a tenant-scoped (BYO) host in Prompt 47 gets 100 %. (b) 46b's lab proof includes a tampered host: a wrong ETag reported → refused; a Worker build that seals with the wrong key → the sample catches it on the first commit. (c) The daily `routing_truth` chunked sample reads codec chunks THROUGH `openEdgeCodecChunk` (the `unreadable` verdict exists), so a poisoned chunk is found within a day, not at a customer's read. |
| **RP68-4** | P4 | The chunk metrics double count: `hit` is incremented at index time AND at commit, `miss` at index and `stored` at commit, so `hit + stored` is not the chunks committed; the reaper counts an expired codec PUT as `retired` and `ReleasedBytes` though nothing was queued. | `edge_codec_control.go:295, 321, 557, 572`; `edge_reaper.go:120–123` | Index-time outcomes `hit` / `miss`; commit-time `referenced` / `stored` / `head_mismatch` / `failed`; the reaper's codec branch counts `metered`-style its own outcome `claims_dropped`. |
| **RP68-5** | P4 → fix now | RB2-3 is cheap to close and will bite the first operator who runs the chunk move after codec rows exist: `chunk_move` verifies `ciphertext_hash` as a SHA-256 of the blob and declares every `AES256-EW` blob damaged (a false `Missing` per codec chunk; no delete, but a report that cannot be trusted). | `chunk_move.go` (the verify), RB2-3 | One branch: an `AES256-EW` row verifies by MD5 (the same `openEdgeCodecChunk` rule); a test with one codec row through the dry run. |
| **RP68-6** | P4 | The HIT path copies the HOST's `size` into the manifest's offsets without comparing it to the row's `size_bytes`; a wrong size from the host shifts every later chunk's offset and breaks range reads of a readable object. The stored path is covered (the sealed length is a function of the size the link was signed for). | `edge_codec_control.go:616–637` | `referenceEdgeChunk` `RETURNING size_bytes` and the commit fails on a mismatch (one line + one test row). |

| RP68-8 | note | Codec rows and origin-path rows of the same tenant never dedup against each other (keyed vs plain identity, `AES256-EW` vs `AES256-CE`): a tenant whose small writes go through the origin and large ones through the edge stores shared chunks twice. ## The residuals, re-checked

- **RB2-1 (P3, stands):** the codec re-home after a rotation is a pass of its own; `key_reencrypt` skips `AES256-EW` rows; nothing reads a codec chunk under the wrong version. Prompt 47 or 48 carries it (the `kind="chunk"` pending gauge must then count them too — today it does not, which hides the backlog; add the count when the pass exists).
- **RB2-2 (P3 → P4):** confirmed narrower than written. The reconcile touches only `ready` rows whose `last_accessed_at` is older than the 7-day grace (`dedup_gc.go:135–164`), and a part's commit stamps `NOW()` on every row it flips or references; an upload live past 7 days with early chunks untouched would have those refs reconciled away, and the next day's reconcile (which runs BEFORE the sweep) restores them once Complete's manifest rows exist. Exposure: an upload active for more than 7 days that completes in the one window between a reconcile and its sweep. The 48 h abandon reaper bounds it. Stays a residual.
- **RB2-3:** RP68-5 closes it.
- **RB2-4 (P4, stands):** the erasure releases manifest refs set-based; `part_chunks` refs of ACTIVE uploads of the erased tenant drift until the rows go with the tenant.
- **RB2-5 (informational, stands).**
- **RB2-6 (P4, found by the Part A session after this review, fixed in #1000):** the three `vaultaire_edge_codec_*` families were declared and pre-created but never `MustRegister`ed, so the prod scrape after #997 showed none of them. This review missed it: it read the vec declarations and not the endpoint. Lesson kept in the prompts: a new metric's pre-creation test reads `/metrics`, not the vec.
- **RB-9 (open):** the first-request 403 after a content-changing Worker deploy; the headers capture is armed for the 0.3.0 deploy (46b Step 0.1).
