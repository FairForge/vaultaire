## What is wrong, measured

A two-hour soak on 2026-11-06 (two 5 TB drives in two tenants, `cmd/tools/onedrive-bench` through the production driver, 111 rounds of 48 × 256 MB PUT then GET at concurrency 24, 666 GB written AND read per drive = 6.7× / 3.3× Microsoft's published hourly rates) showed how Microsoft answers over-rate traffic: **not a cap but bursts** — about one minute of `HTTP 429` every five minutes, on the 8 MiB CDN ranges of reads (19 of 111 rounds lost ≥ 20 of 48 objects; 574 range 429s) and sometimes on upload-session fragments (9 rounds of write failures); the round after each burst ran at full speed (PUT 436 / GET 642 MB/s for two drives). A client that waits out a burst loses nothing. Ours loses the object:

1. **Reads give a range two attempts** (`odAttempts` on a request path, `onedrive.go` ~`:1091` `graphDo`'s rule; a `Retry-After` is honoured only up to `odMaxRetryWait` = 30 s, line 70) and the whole-object read of ≥ 10 MiB is `parfetch.Start` over `fetchRange` (lines 544–570: a non-206 is `onedrive range %d-%d: HTTP %d, not 206`). A burst outlasts two attempts.
2. **A refused range can end the body CLEANLY.** 135 times in the soak the bench reported `size mismatch: got 0 want 268435456`: `Get` returned a reader that delivered 0 bytes and then `io.EOF` with no error. Find why in `parfetch.NewReader(stream, nil)` (line 552) and `internal/parfetch/stream.go` `Reader.Read` (lines ~552–567) / `Stream.Next` (459–472): a part whose fetch failed must make `Read` return that error, never `io.EOF`. The API's integrity reader (`object_integrity.go`) would cut such a response for a customer GET; a shard read inside the parity rebuild (`vault_parity_read.go`) and `blob-verify` must see an error.
3. **Writes give a fragment the same bounded retries** (`putChunk`, line 1419: "onedrive chunk exhausted retries: onedrive chunk HTTP 429" in the soak), and a failed fragment fails the object although the session is still open and `nextExpectedRanges` would resume it.
4. **Nothing paces.** The driver sends as fast as the caller does; the per-user line (3,000 requests / 5 min; ~8 parallel streams per drive before 429s in the 2026-11-05 single-drive run) and the published volumes (50 GB/h in, 100 GB/h out per user; 400 GB/h per app per tenant) are known and unenforced. `RateLimit-*` headers (`trackRateLimit`, line 1176) are recorded and acted on by nothing — and were never once sent in 1,200 small writes or the soaks.
5. **One slow shard stretches a rebuild**: one of ~10 parity reads of a 1 GiB object ran at 17 MB/s against 78–101 for the others, on three drives (2026-11-04) and on six (2026-11-05). Nothing hedges a slow range.

The operator page `docs/FLEET.md` has the whole table; the good-tenant posture it states is: pace at the PUBLISHED rates, treat a 429 burst as the budget's signal, ride it.

## Decisions (made — do not reopen)

- **D-F3-1 A refused or failed range fails the body.** `parfetch` surfaces a part's final error through `Reader.Read` (and `Stream.Next`) as that error — never `io.EOF`, never a short clean end. Pinned by a test on `parfetch` alone (a part that fails after N retries → `Read` returns the error after the bytes before it) and by a driver test (a fake CDN that 429s one range of a 32 MiB object → `Get`'s reader returns an error, `io.Copy` ≠ nil). Applies to every `parfetch` user (the chunked GET, the large GETs) — check their tests still hold; it is a correctness fix for all of them.
- **D-F3-2 Ranges and fragments get `odBurstAttempts` = 6 attempts** with `Retry-After` honoured up to `odMaxRetryWait` raised to **90 s** (a burst lasts about a minute), every wait on `ctx`; a GET's first-byte bound (25 s) and the fragment's answer bound (2 min) are per attempt and unchanged. A whole read may therefore take minutes under a burst — that is the trade (an error is worse; the caller's context still bounds it). `common.WithJob` keeps its 3 → 8.
- **D-F3-3 A fragment that fails after its attempts resumes the session** from `nextExpectedRanges` (GET the upload URL) instead of failing the object, once per fragment; a session that is gone (404) restarts the upload only if the body is rewindable (the pooled fragment buffer is; a streamed unknown-length body is not → the error it is today).
- **D-F3-4 A slow range is hedged**: after `max(2 s, 3 × the stream's median range time)` a range not answered is requested again on a fresh connection to the SAME download URL; the first verified copy wins, the loser is cancelled (`parfetch`'s hedge exists for the chunked GET — `CHUNK_GET_HEDGE_AFTER`; wire it for the fleet's ranges with `PERMAFROST_RANGE_HEDGE_AFTER`, default 2 s, `0` = off). Never a hedge of a fragment PUT.
- **D-F3-5 Pacing, per drive and per tenant, in the driver**: a token bucket per account for requests (`PERMAFROST_ACCOUNT_RPS`, default 8 req/s, burst 100 — the 3,000 / 5 min line), a byte bucket per account for ingress and one for egress (`PERMAFROST_ACCOUNT_IN_GBH` 50, `PERMAFROST_ACCOUNT_OUT_GBH` 100 — the published rates; `0` = unpaced), and one per TENANT (app registration) for each direction (`PERMAFROST_TENANT_GBH` 400). A caller waits on the bucket (never a refusal; the wait is bounded by its ctx); back-pressure on the source for uploads (the `sia_write.go` pattern: the fragment is read through the bucket before it is sent, so the fragment deadline times Microsoft only). Buckets are keyed on the account's identity (`identity()`), so six slots in three tenants share three tenant buckets.
- **D-F3-6 A 429 is the budget's signal**: on a 429 (any op) the account's buckets are drained to zero for the `Retry-After` (default 5 s) so the whole process backs off that drive, not just the one request (`throttledUntil`, line 160, already exists — make every bucket read it). `RateLimit-Remaining` < 20 % of `RateLimit-Limit`, when the headers appear, drains likewise for `RateLimit-Reset`.
- **D-F3-7 Decorate traffic**: `odUserAgent` (line 1116) becomes `ISV|Stored|permafrost/<BuildSHA>` (Microsoft's documented format for prioritised traffic).
- **D-F3-8 Metrics**, registered in `prom_metrics.go` (declared is not registered — a `/metrics` presence test): `vaultaire_permafrost_throttles_total{account,op}` (429s), `vaultaire_permafrost_paced_seconds_total{account,dir}` (time waited on buckets), `vaultaire_permafrost_range_hedges_total{outcome}`, `vaultaire_permafrost_fragment_resumes_total{outcome}`; rule `PermafrostThrottling` (warn: > 10 throttles/min on one account for 10 min).
- **D-F3-9 No change to placement, fragment size, range size, drive selection, delete semantics, or the slot scheme** (WP-FLEET-4/5/11).

## When a step does not go as written

- `parfetch`'s fix changes the chunked GET's behaviour in a way its tests do not expect: stop and write down what it did before (a chunk that failed may have been served as a short clean body there too — that would be a second bug of the same kind; fix both, test both).
- The session `nextExpectedRanges` resume is refused by Microsoft for a fragment the session already holds: skip that fragment (the session's view wins), continue from the next expected range.
- Pacing at the published rates starves the parity job's protect (its budget is time-boxed): raise the per-account defaults by env on prod and say so in the PR — the knobs exist for exactly that; never hard-code a higher default without a measurement.
- Anything that needs the owner (prod `.env` knob values beyond the defaults, the fleet accounts 1–3 in a soak): stop and ask.
