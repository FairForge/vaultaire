## Verdict

**HOLDS at P2.** Every RP74 fix landed with its test, and three of them
better than asked (the gate's shape check costs nothing, the trailer is
verified beside the backend's PUT, the node refuses a public bind without
TLS). The mirror is the right shape — one address on two legs, the leg
decided once at the admit, the copy never the client's failure, the job the
net under it — and tenant zero's 94 GB got its second copy on prod the same
day. The seal-by-default switch is built the safe way round (a write that
cannot be sealed is a 503, the codec grant creates a new tenant's first key
version). The catalogue holds contract line 5 by construction. Two P2s
remain, both in the mirror and both live for tenant zero: **the leg is chosen
with no regard for the bucket's residency or tier** (RP76-1 — latent on prod
only because every bucket is `us-central-1`), and **a mirrored write runs at
the slower leg's pace until a fixed deadline cuts the mirror** (RP76-2 — a
stalled Lyve PUT, the 2026-10-17 shape, is now a 60 s client stall and an
aws-cli retry). Four P3s and four P4s follow. RP76-1 and RP76-2 are the
first two items of Prompt 49's Part 0.

| **RP76-1** | P2 | **The mirror leg is chosen with no regard for the bucket's residency or its tier.** `mirrorLegFor(placement)` takes the first leg of `MIRROR_LEGS` that is a registered EdgeWriter and not the placement's backend — nothing reads the bucket's `region` (5.14.7: an EU bucket's objects stay on `idrive-eu-*`; the GDPR page says so) or its `tier_preference` (a strict target the customer chose: `sia`, `blomp`, `sync`). A mirrored tenant's EU bucket gets its second copy on `lyve` us-west-1 at the admit and in the backfill, silently; a Blomp-tier object (unsealed on prod, plain HTTP) is backfilled onto Lyve. Latent on prod today only because every bucket is `us-central-1` and tenant zero's buckets are `resilient` (Lyve → iDrive is the intended case). | `edge_mirror.go` `mirrorLegFor`, `blob_copies_job.go` `backfill` | The leg must satisfy the bucket: for a region-pinned bucket only a leg in the same jurisdiction (`bucketRegionDriver`'s region → a leg whose driver is that region's, or an `idrive-<region>` of the same jurisdiction; today the EU has no second fast leg, so **no mirror, never a refusal**, and the bucket page and `docs/API.md` say "single copy" for it); a strict-target tier (`engine` strict backends) is never mirrored by the admit or the backfill; `resilient` and `auto` mirror as now. Tests: an EU bucket's write gets one link and no copy row; the backfill skips a `blomp` bucket; a `us-central-1` bucket mirrors. |
| **RP76-2** | P2 | **A mirrored write runs at the slower leg's pace until a fixed deadline cuts the mirror — live for tenant zero.** Step 0.1 proved workerd's `tee()` is backpressured (the fast branch waits for the slow one), and the Go tee writes each 1 MiB buffer to both pipes before the next read. So a client's PUT throughput is min(primary, mirror), and the mirror is cut only at `30 s + body / 2 MiB/s` (`mirrorDeadlineMs`) — a 64 MiB part on a stalled leg holds the primary for 62 s, a 1 GiB whole PUT for 9 minutes, and a leg crawling at 1.9 MiB/s is never cut at all. Lyve us-east-1 answered nothing to 1–3 % of PUTs on 2026-10-17 and timed out mid-backfill on 2026-10-28 (46 retries in the note): each such part is now a ≥ 60 s stall at the client, past aws-cli's 60 s read timeout, so the client retries the part and the bytes go twice. The closeout measured no mirrored throughput; the SPEED bench's "tee = pass-through" had two equal sinks. The Go tee's single 30 s `mirrorStall` is the same shape with a shorter fuse. | `edge/s3-ingest/src/codec.js` `putTwo`/`mirrorDeadlineMs`, `internal/dataplane/mirror.go` `mirrorTee.Write` | The mirror must never slow the primary beyond a bounded lag: (a) in the Worker, cut the mirror when the primary has finished and the mirror has not answered within 5 s, and while both run when the mirror lags the primary by more than 16 MiB (count bytes through a `TransformStream` on each branch — the bytes are already in the isolate's budget); (b) in Go, give the mirror pipe a 16 MiB ring buffer and cut it when the lag exceeds it, never block `Write` on the mirror beyond the buffer; (c) measure on prod: a 256 MiB and a 1 GiB `aws s3 cp` into tenant zero's bucket with `edge_mirror` on and off, the numbers in the note; (d) `vaultaire_blob_copies_total{outcome="cut"}` for mirrors cut loose. Until (a)–(b) land, say in the docs that a mirrored write runs at the slower leg. |
