## Decisions

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D-P24-1 | **One refund, one row (RP28-1):** a refund of a charge whose provider records its own refunds (`payments.RecordsOwnRefunds` = Stripe) is CREATED by an operator only under the provider's `re_…` id (`ErrRefundProviderRef`, 400, no credit-note number spent); `payment_id` may name the charge or a refund row; a refund already in the ledger is `ErrRefundRecorded` for the webhook (`Refund.FromProvider`) and an idempotent hit for an operator (200, `existing`). | The webhook is Stripe's record of its refunds; the form now meets it on the same `(provider, provider_id)` instead of minting `refund:<uuid>`. Either order gives one row. |
| D-P24-2 | **Shorten from a recorded refund, once:** the operator names the refund (its `re_…` or its row) with *shorten* and the amount blank → `paid_through` moves back by that refund's share of the charge's period; the refund row is stamped `metadata.shortened` and a second shorten is `ErrRefundShortened` (409); audited `billing.paid_through_adjusted` (no new refund row → no second `billing.refund_recorded`). A refund shortened at creation is stamped too. Departure from § 7: the admin tool records Stripe refunds only by naming the webhook's row. | Keeps "shorten" reachable for a Stripe refund in either order without a second row, and makes "once" structural (the stamp lives on the refund). |
| D-P24-3 | **RP28-2:** `recordInvoicePaid` lists the charge's refunds only while the invoice's ledger row is not settled yet (one Stripe call; a listing failure answers 500 BEFORE anything is written) and records the succeeded ones when the delivery NEWLY settles it; `charge.refund.updated` (succeeded) records from the fetched charge and Stripe's refund list now. A recording failure after the commit is an Error line naming the refunds (the redelivery is a replay). | "Only when Newly" with no second Stripe call on replays; the fetched state, never the payload's status. |
| D-P24-4 | **The lab wins over the design** where they differ (§ 3.2 "Verified", § 3.3 Lab rows): dedup on `originalDeliveryId` (always set); an unknown invoice is BTCPay's **403 `missing-permission`** (no 404) → 200 + Warn; readiness reads `GET …/invoices?take=1` (the scoped key cannot GET the store) and the health BODY; **money from the invoice's USD `paidAmount`**, never the crypto `due` (it grows by a network fee after a partial payment); **no grant on a hand-marked Settled with `paidAmount` < amount**; Processing is informational (a double spend sends it back to New with no event). | Every protocol fact in § 3 was from the OpenAPI templates; the lab proved six of them wrong or incomplete. |
| D-P24-5 | **All writes of a delivery in the claim's transaction:** the claim, the ledger row and the entitlement commit together under `payments.Lock`; any failure rolls all back and BTCPay redelivers. The grant happens only on a NEW settle (a replay or a later event of a settled invoice changes nothing). | § 2.4's "lock the tenant in the claim" — and a grant computed from "now" must never run twice. |
| D-P24-6 | **The pending row is committed BEFORE the invoice exists**, under a provisional id `pending:<ledger uuid>` (in the provider's own transaction, never the guard's), then renamed to the invoice id; if the rename is lost, the webhook adopts the row from the invoice's `metadata.payment_id` (same tenant). A failed create marks the row `failed`. | Never an invoice the ledger does not know (the customer could pay it and be "foreign"); the crash window becomes a self-healing rename. |
| D-P24-7 | **`payments.LockedCheckout`** (`StartCheckoutTx(ctx, tx, c)`): `StartCheckout` hands its guard transaction to a provider that implements it. BTCPay grants an order the tenant's ledger credit covers in full there — a $0 charge settled **unnumbered** (RP24-1's rule), no invoice. Departure: `Checkout.CreditCents` is not trusted; the provider reads `payments.CreditBalance` from the ledger. | The guard holds the entitlement lock on its connection: a provider that took it on another would wait on itself. |
| D-P24-8 | **Credits as rows that track a number:** `partial:<invoice>` = what arrived on an invoice that did not settle (grows with a late partial payment; set to 0 if the invoice settles late after all), `overpaid:<invoice>` = paid − amount; balance = credit rows − `credit_cents` consumed by settled charges, never negative. | A late full payment after an expired partial must not be both a credit and a grant. |
| D-P24-9 | **The paid-late automation compares USD `paidAmount ≥ amount`** at the invoice's rate (BTCPay records no then-rate). Departure from § 3.3's "at the then-rate". | The only rate BTCPay gives is the invoice's; a customer who pays the BTC amount late is paid in full by BTCPay's own measure. |
| D-P24-10 | **Transport copied, not imported:** the client uses a small transport with the `TunedHTTPClient` knobs that matter (10 s dial / TLS / header / end-to-end, small pool, TLS 1.2+), no redirects; one retry on a fresh connection for a GET that got no answer, never a POST. | `drivers` does not import `payments` (no cycle), but the payments tree should not link the storage drivers. |
| D-P24-11 | **The webhook secret** is read like the API key (credential `btcpay_webhook_secret` → `_FILE` → env). | Both are secrets; one rule. |
