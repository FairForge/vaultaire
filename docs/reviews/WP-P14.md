## Decisions

1. **A chunk PUT outlives its request (RP14-1).** The pool cancelled every chunk PUT in flight when a chunked upload ended, and a cancel after the body went out was RP13-1's unanswered case: the claim stayed for the 3 min window. An interrupted upload left up to eight such claims and the client's immediate retry waited on all of them. Now a PUT that has started runs to its own end on `context.WithoutCancel` + `chunkPutBound`; the worker leaves at once when its request ends (the handler answers; a sibling's failure no longer waits 90 s) and the PUT's goroutine settles the claim by what the backend said — a one-shot handoff (`decided`) decides which side owns the result:
   - answered OK → `readyChunk(…, takeRef=false)`: `ready` at ref 0. The aborted write took no reference (or gave it back through the pool's compensation), so a retry of the same body dedups onto it within seconds, and a blob nobody retries is dedup GC's after its grace;
   - answered with an error → the claim is dropped (today's rule);
   - no answer → the claim is kept (today's rule, now the rare case).
   A chunk whose PUT had not started when the request ended is never attempted: `storeOne` and `submit` check the pool context, and a claim taken between the check and the PUT is dropped at once. The live path's `readyChunk` runs on its own 10 s clock too: a request that ends between the backend's answer and the flip must not leave a landed blob behind a claim.
2. **The drain waits for chunk PUTs after the long operations.** Every chunk PUT is registered (`chunkPutRegistry`); `Server.Shutdown` runs `drainChunkPuts` after `drainLongOps` (a detached Complete's chunked branch holds chunk PUTs of its own) and before the trackers flush and the engine closes the database, bounded by `chunkPutBound` + the 10 s finish + 5 s — never near the 15 min long-op bound. `/health` names them to loopback as `chunk_puts_in_flight`, and `vaultaire-switch` adds that to `long_ops_in_flight` (a build before this has no field: 0).
3. **The routing gauges export every backend of the last run (RP14-12).** The collector emitted a backend's `missing_ratio` only when the run had a verdict on it (present + missing > 0). On prod the one backend with rows, `idrive` (501), had 10 consecutive errors and no verdict, so no ratio series existed at all and `RoutingTruthMissing` / `RoutingTruthMissingJump` watched nothing. Now every backend of the result has its ratio (0 without a verdict), its `rows`, its `verdicts` (present + missing) and its `errors`; `RoutingTruthNoVerdict` (warning) fires on rows > 0 with verdicts == 0 — prod's `idrive` since 2026-10-11; the NULL-backend count of `object_head_cache` and `object_versions` is a series at 0 when there is none. This is not a restart-shaped gap: the series were absent on every slot since WP-R7-5 whenever no backend had a verdict.
4. **A `-N` object is verified against the whole body's MD5 (A.4).** Complete's whole branch tees the staged-parts stream (the plaintext's, before the sealer — the tee adds no read) into an MD5 recorded as `body_md5` on the head row and, through `copyHeadAttrsToVersionSQL`, on the version row; the whole re-encrypt re-points rows and keeps it. Every whole 200 — `HandleGet` (head row or `?versionId` row), `/cdn` — arms the integrity reader with `integrityDigest(body_md5, etag)`: the digest when the row has one, else the ETag. The ETag stays `-N`.
5. **CopyObject of a `-N` source is a single-part object.** Its ETag is the MD5 of the bytes it wrote (as on AWS, a copy under 5 GiB gets a single-part ETag), so `body_md5` stays NULL and the copy is verified by its ETag. PUT, generated objects and the R2 direct upload leave it NULL too (their ETag is the MD5); an R2 direct multipart upload stays `unverified{multipart}` (R2 gives no whole MD5). A row written before 094 is served exactly as before.
6. **The SSE-C key's MD5 is kept with the object (RP14-11).** `crypto.SSECKeyMD5(key)` — the base64 MD5 the request's `-key-MD5` header carried and the write verified; never the key — is written by every SSE-C write to `ssec_key_md5` on both rows. HEAD and GET with a key of another MD5 answer **403 `AccessDenied`** from the row (`ssecKeyMismatch`, next to Prompt 13's 400), before any backend call and with no object header; `?versionId` checks the version row's. A request whose SSE-C headers do not parse is left to the opener's 400. A row with no MD5 keeps today's answers (HEAD 200, GET 403 at the blob). The `docs/API.md` deviation row is scoped to objects written before 2026-10-19.
7. **A Geyser PUT with a known length streams in one attempt (WP-R7-2).** Every Vault write path passes `engine.WithContentLength` (verified: PUT `s3_engine_adapter.go:1231`, Complete's whole branch `s3_multipart.go:985`, CopyObject `s3_copy.go:469`, the parity shards `vault_parity.go:875`, a Smart demotion's copy to tape `smart_demotion.go:422` and the promotion back `smart_promotion.go:437`, the re-encrypt `key_reencrypt_whole.go:261`, generated objects `background_put.go:263`, the canary `backend_canary.go:91`, the chunk move `chunk_move.go:531`). `putStreamed`: Content-Length from the caller, the payload unsigned (the SDK cannot hash a stream it has not read; it is what the S3 CLIs send), checksums `WhenRequired` (an unseekable body would otherwise go aws-chunked with a trailing CRC32, which Vail does not take), `RetryMaxAttempts` 1. **What changes on a retry:** a materialised body was seekable, so the SDK re-sent a stalled PUT (two attempts, measured); a stream cannot be rewound, so neither the SDK's retry nor a fresh-connection retry — the engine reports a failure after bytes went out as `ErrNoFailover` (for the non-rewindable bodies every Vault caller passes) and an unanswered attempt as one that may still land (RP13-1). **Which bound applies:** Geyser's own PUT-answer bound (`geyserOpTimeouts.Put`, 5 min: tape) and the caller's context; the per-size `FIXED_BUCKET_PUT_TIMEOUT` deadline and its retry are the fixed-bucket and Lyve drivers'. An unknown length still materialises, counted `vaultaire_geyser_put_materialized_total{reason="unknown_length"}` (0 on prod).
8. **The chunked-write budget (WP-R2-6).** One `chunkPutBudget` per process (`CHUNK_PUT_BUDGET_BYTES`: a quarter of `GOMEMLIMIT` when one is set — `debug.SetMemoryLimit(-1)` — else 2 GiB; 256 MiB–64 GiB; Warn-and-default). A chunked write's first act, before a byte of the body is read or a chunk claimed, is to reserve one worker's worst-case buffers (`chunkPutReserveBytes` = 3 × `crypto.ChunkMaxSize` = 48 MiB) within **2 s** (`chunkPutBudgetWait`); otherwise **503 `SlowDown` + `Retry-After: 30`** before anything is stored (the claim path has not run), from `HandlePut` and `writeCompleteStoreError`, counted in `vaultaire_chunk_put_budget_refusals_total` **and in `vaultaire_errors_total`** — real saturation, unlike the egress guard's forgeable per-client 503 (WP-R10-9 PM-1), so the 5xx page sees it. A write that holds its reserve always progresses: every chunk's buffers (3 × its size, the worst case) come from what the write holds, else the budget, else the dispatcher waits for the write's own chunks to finish — fewer in flight, down to one, never a refusal. A chunk's hold shrinks to its real buffers once compressed and sealed, returns when its store ends, and a PUT that outlives its request keeps its blob's share until it ends. The chunker's look-ahead is bounded apart: `crypto.ChunkContextDepth(…, 2)` — ≤ 3 chunks + its 16 MiB work buffer (≤ 64 MiB per write) outside the budget; it was 10 deep (≤ 192 MiB).
9. **The structure test names every large allocation.** `TestDataPathBounds_EveryLargeAllocationIsUnderABudget` walks every `make([]byte, N)` in `api`, `crypto`, `parfetch`, `drivers` and `engine` whose N is not a literal under 1 MiB (27 sites) and demands a row naming its budget or per-stream bound. Under the PUT budget: the chunker's chunks and the sealed chunk; under the GET budget: the parallel ranged and sealed readers; bounded per stream: the streaming sealer/opener (≤ 2 MiB), SSE-C, Geyser's ranges (≤ 64 MiB per GET), the parity stripe (8 × 1 MiB), the Sync bridge's retryable bodies (≤ 8 MiB), the fleet's upload fragment. **The named residual:** the S3 uploader (`putSinglePartIfSmall` ≤ 16 MiB and the manager's 8 × 16 MiB parts per whole-object write to idrive / lyve / wasabi / r2 / s3compat) is bounded per request and unbounded in number — RP15-3.
10. **The unit's memory lines are an owner item.** `systemctl cat vaultaire@8001` on the box: no `MemoryHigh=`, no `MemoryMax=`, no `GOMEMLIMIT`; `free -g`: 121 GiB, 4 used. `docs/DEPLOY.md` § Memory recommends `Environment=GOMEMLIMIT=40GiB`, `MemoryHigh=48G`, `MemoryMax=56G` per slot (two slots overlap in a switch), not installed.
