## The rule

`tenant_quotas` and every `tenant_floor_quotas` row equal the billed bytes
(`usage/billed.go`) at every committed instant. The billed bytes are:
- the head rows;
- the retained versions;
- what every active multipart upload holds.

What a write or a delete changes in the ledger is written in the same
transaction that changes the rows. It is the last statement before COMMIT.
Nothing about the bill happens after a COMMIT. A reservation made before
a write is the only exception, and a write that commits nothing gives it
back.

## What was built

**Part A: every release in the transaction that makes it true (#718).**
- **Writes.** `commitBlobWrite` settles the write's `writeBill` last, before COMMIT (`writeBill.settleTx` → `usage.ApplyLedgerTx`). The settle:
  - charges the head row's measured size on its floor;
  - takes back the reservation on the floor it was made;
  - releases what the displaced row frees (`displacedRow.releases`: the WP-R2-1 C2 rule, where a blob a version keeps keeps its bill).

  This covers PUT (whole, chunked, sealed), CopyObject (whole, chunked), CompleteMultipartUpload, generated objects and direct upload.
- **Deletes.** Every delete releases in its own transaction: DELETE, DeleteObjects, the delete marker, `DELETE ?versionId` (`deleteVersion`), a Suspended null rewrite, the deletion runner and the export expiry. They go through `deleteHeadRowTx` and `releasesTx`.
- **Removed.** `settlePutQuota`, `releaseDisplacedOn` and `releaseDetached` are gone. `TestLedger_NoQuotaStatementAfterACommit` (AST) fails if one comes back, or if `releases()` is read outside the commit's settle.
- **Lock order** (`blob_flip.go`, `usage/ledger_tx.go`): key advisory lock → head row → the key's version lock and rows → the upload row → `tenant_quotas` → `tenant_floor_quotas` (standard, then vault). The ledger comes last, so the quota row is held from its UPDATE to COMMIT only. Nothing that holds it waits for a key, row or upload lock: the reservation, the reconcile and SetHouse take the quota rows alone, in the same order. A commit that leaves the total unchanged (a new key whose reservation equals its size) never locks the quota row.

**Part B: net reservation on overwrite (#718).**
- A write first reserves its full size. Only a refusal asks what its commit would release on the same floor (`predictRelease`), then reserves the difference: nothing when the write shrinks the key.
- `predictRelease` is **`commitBlobWrite` as a dry run** (every statement executed, then rolled back). The reservation and the commit decide by one rule, never two.
- The commit's settle is the correction. It refuses (`errQuotaRace` → 403, nothing committed, the new blob an `orphan_write`) only when the credited bytes were not released and the ledger would end past its limit.
- A versioned overwrite gets no credit (the old bytes stay). A floor change gets no credit on the new floor. PUT, CopyObject and the chunked copy use it.

**Part C: multipart parts are reserved (#719, 091).**
- **091** adds `multipart_uploads.reserved_bytes` and `reserved_floor`, plus a partial index on active holdings.
- **UploadPart** reserves its declared bytes, less what its part number holds, before the first byte is staged (`usage.ReserveTx`, under the upload row's lock; 403, nothing staged). The install, the part row and the settle to the measured size are one transaction. A cut part gives its reservation back.
- **Complete** takes what the upload holds into the object's bill inside its commit (`writeBill.Held`). Unlisted parts are released.
- **Abort, the reaper and the erasure** release exactly what the row holds, via `endUploadReleasing`.
- **The reconcile, SetHouse and `BilledBytes`** count active holdings.
- **Dashboard.** The single-total "Storage Used" card shows the uploads-in-progress share.
- **The staging guard** (`MULTIPART_STAGING_MIN_FREE`) answers 503 SlowDown when a part would leave the staging filesystem below its floor. It has a counter, a gauge and `deploy/monitoring/vaultaire-multipart.yml`.

**Part D: `quota_usage_events` off the hot path; R10-19 (#719).**
- Nothing writes the table any more.
- `GET /api/v1/user/quota/history` is `bandwidth_usage_daily` (uploaded / downloaded / requests per UTC day). OpenAPI is updated.
- The overview's Recent Activity is the latest writes from the head rows.
- **R10-19.** SetHouse seeds a new floor row by the reconcile's computation under the quota row's lock. A write whose reservation predates that row is charged there in full at commit (`Reservation.Floor` → `writeBill.FloorReserved`): the window is closed.

## Decisions (for the owner)

1. **The reconcile counts uploads by the row's `reserved_bytes`, not the sum of `multipart_parts`.** The row includes a part still streaming (its up-front reservation). It is written under the upload row's lock in the same transaction as the ledger, so the two agree at every instant, and it survives a restart. The parts' sum would free a part in flight.
2. **Usage history and activity.**
   - `GET /api/v1/user/quota/history` now returns the metered daily traffic (`uploaded`, `downloaded`, `requests`). That is a shape change: `peak_usage` and `deleted` are gone, but they had only ever returned a reservation size and 0.
   - The overview lists the latest uploads. Deletes are not listed (no table records them truthfully).
   - The `quota_usage_events` table stays until its drop (R108-5).
3. **Customer-visible at the quota edge:**
   - an overwrite with a smaller or equal object is accepted at 100 %;
   - parts in progress count against the quota, and the free tier's part that would pass it is refused at the part, not at Complete;
   - "used" includes uploads in progress, with a line saying so on the single-total card;
   - a failed Complete keeps the upload's parts reserved until the retry, Abort or the 48 h reaper.
4. **`MULTIPART_STAGING_MIN_FREE` default = max(10 %, min(50 GiB, 50 %))** instead of max(10 %, 50 GiB). Prod stages on `/tmp`, a 61 GiB tmpfs (half the box's 121 GiB RAM), where 50 GiB would leave 11 GiB for every upload in flight; this gives 30 GiB. It equals the prompt's rule on any filesystem of 100 GiB or more. The real fix is WP-R3-5: stage on the data disk.
5. **The 100 %-floor case was not run on prod.** The only audited route that sets a tenant's limit is the dashboard's admin tenants page (`admin.tenant_quota_set`), behind the owner's MFA. The local proof stands.
